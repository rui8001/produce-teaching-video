# Lesson design

## Define the learner change

Before writing, record:

- learner and prerequisite knowledge;
- one learning objective stated as something the learner can explain, predict, recognize, or perform;
- the likely wrong prediction, missing mental image, or failed procedural step;
- the evidence or demonstration that can change that belief;
- one transfer task that is similar enough to be fair but different enough to test understanding.

Split the lesson when two objectives require different explanations or assessments.

## Source contract

Create a source ledger. For every important claim, distinguish:

- primary or authoritative source;
- observed demonstration or real data;
- interpretation;
- simplified or idealized model;
- unresolved fact.

Show the conditions under which a model or procedure works. A clean animation is not experimental evidence, a generated interface is not a product demonstration, and a correct result without reproducible steps is not a tutorial.

## Script test

The script is ready only when a learner can answer:

1. What changed or what action was taken?
2. Why did that produce the result?
3. What term, rule, or step names the relationship?
4. Where would the same idea apply next?

Avoid labels that blame the learner. Make confusion observable and repairable.
